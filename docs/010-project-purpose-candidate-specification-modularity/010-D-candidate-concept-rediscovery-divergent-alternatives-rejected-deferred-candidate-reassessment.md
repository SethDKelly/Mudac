# Purpose

Perform a genuine candidate-concept rediscovery pass after the current project and purpose model have been revalidated.

010-D asks:

> If MUDAC were conceptually rediscovered today from its current purpose obligations, actor needs, success situations and tensions—without being shown the existing sixteen-Concept catalog first—what plausible behavioral concepts and materially different decompositions would we generate, and which earlier rejected/deferred candidates now deserve renewed pressure testing?

This subgroup is intentionally divergent before it is convergent.

It does **not** decide the final Concept catalog. It produces a broad-enough but tractable candidate pressure set for 010-E behavioral specification, while preserving rejected/deferred rationale where a subject is clearly a feature, role, projection, policy, mechanism or implementation noun.

# Governing discovery discipline

This subgroup follows the Base candidate-discovery contract and Daniel Jackson's divergence/convergence posture:

1. derive candidate seeds from purpose, actors, success situations, tensions and counterfactual decompositions;
2. delay comparison with the incumbent sixteen until an independent candidate space exists;
3. seek materially different allocations of behavior, not merely synonyms for current nouns;
4. use Concept criteria as lenses during convergence rather than filters that suppress early divergence;
5. keep unresolved boundary questions visible for 010-E–G rather than forcing premature certainty;
6. keep exploratory/rejected candidates in phase history rather than polluting canonical Concept knowledge;
7. do not use architecture, source topology, persistence, authentication providers, AWS services or frozen implementation structure as candidate evidence.

The candidate labels in this record are working labels only. They are not canonical Concept names, stable rule identifiers or implementation module names.

# Anti-anchoring protocol

To make rediscovery substantive rather than ceremonial, candidate generation was performed in this order:

```text
Project mandate + actors
        ↓
P-01..P-09 purpose obligations
        ↓
S-01..S-12 representative success situations
        ↓
T-01..T-10 tensions / distributional guardrails
        ↓
independent candidate seeds
        ↓
material alternative decompositions
        ↓
only then compare with incumbent Concepts / mechanisms / prior rejections
```

A candidate that resembles an incumbent Concept is not accepted because of that resemblance. Conversely, a challenger is not favored merely because it is novel.

# Divergent candidate generation before incumbent comparison

## A. Competition context and competitor structure

### A1 — Competition Context / Governed Event

**Purpose pressure:** P-04, P-06, P-08.

**Behavior sketch:** establish one bounded competition occurrence, its governing lifecycle/policies, current operational state, and the authority by which official closeout occurs.

**Why it may be a Concept:** users reason about "this competition" as a persistent governed context in which participation, judging and outcome authority happen.

**Concern:** an umbrella context can become a dumping ground for unrelated state. Later specificity must prove that this is a coherent concept rather than merely scope shared by other concepts.

### A2 — Competitor / Team

**Purpose pressure:** P-02, P-06, P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** represent the competing student unit whose work is evaluated and whose competition treatment/outcome must remain historically identifiable.

**Why it may be a Concept:** stable user-facing mental construct with lifecycle and correction behavior independent of any particular score or rank.

**Concern:** avoid collapsing competition-facing identity, classification or outcome state into the competitor itself.

### A3 — Competitive Classification / Cohort

**Purpose pressure:** P-02, P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** establish a declared population within which Teams are meaningfully compared or eligibility rules differ.

**Why it may be a Concept:** some competitions explicitly operate recognizable divisions/categories with assignment and correction behavior.

**Concern:** it may be optional configuration, policy classification or generic tagging rather than an independently valuable concept in every MUDAC variant.

### A4 — Competition-Facing Identity / Blind Identity

**Purpose pressure:** P-02, P-08.

**Behavior sketch:** present a stable context-appropriate identity for a Team while withholding bias-sensitive administrative identity from ordinary judging.

**Why it may be a Concept:** the pseudonymous identity has user-visible continuity and controlled resolution behavior.

**Concern:** could instead be a contextual representation/disclosure rule of Team. Later behavioral specification must show whether it owns meaningful independent state/actions.

## B. Human participation and authority

### B1 — Human Identity Continuity

**Purpose pressure:** P-03, P-07, P-08.

**Behavior sketch:** preserve continuity that actions across participation episodes belong to the same human without making role or authority permanent.

**Why it may be a Concept:** returning-person recognition, attribution and correction depend on continuity distinct from event role.

**Concern:** it may be generic account/identity support rather than application Concept unless the product-specific behavior is sufficiently user-facing and semantic.

### B2 — Competition Participation / Capacity

**Purpose pressure:** P-03, P-04, P-08.

**Behavior sketch:** represent a person's time-bounded involvement in one Competition in a particular capacity such as Judge or Organizer.

**Why it may be a Concept:** enrollment/check-in/withdrawal/completion are user-recognizable event behaviors distinct from permanent identity.

**Concern:** some of this may collapse into an Actor Context or Assignment model if separate lifecycle value is weak.

### B3 — Contextual Capability / Disclosure

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-02, P-08, P-09.

**Behavior sketch:** determine what the current actor may do or see given role/capacity, resource relationship, lifecycle state, purpose and time; support explicit exceptional grants/revocation where necessary.

**Why it may be a Concept:** users experience meaningful temporary access/disclosure boundaries and explicit grant/revocation behavior.

**Concern:** ordinary permission checks may be policy/composition rather than a standalone Concept. The candidate must not become RBAC or authentication technology in disguise.

### B4 — Actor Context (merge challenger)

**Purpose pressure:** P-03, P-08.

**Behavior sketch:** combine identity continuity, event participation and current authority into one context object representing "who I am here and what I may currently do."

**Why explore it:** tests whether `Identity + Participation + Access` are over-factored for user meaning.

**Concern:** likely conflates permanent continuity, temporary capacity and contextual permission, making history/correction difficult. Retained as a merge challenger, not a preferred answer.

## C. Evaluation setup, work and judgment

### C1 — Evaluation Basis / Instrument

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-02, P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** define the declared criteria and scoring semantics against which human judgment is expressed, with change/history behavior when the basis legitimately evolves.

**Why it may be a Concept:** judges and organizers recognize the evaluation instrument as a repeatable object with independent authority.

### C2 — Individual Judgment / Evaluation

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-05, P-06, P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** preserve one evaluator's independent assessment, work-in-progress, assertion/finalization, amendment and authorship.

**Why it may be a Concept:** this is the clearest end-to-end user behavior in the product.

**Concern:** avoid absorbing assignment, evaluation basis, provenance or aggregation into the judgment itself.

### C3 — Evaluator Group / Panel

**Purpose pressure:** P-04.

**Behavior sketch:** maintain a reusable intended grouping of evaluators who commonly judge together during the live event.

**Why it may be a Concept:** organizers reason about panels as durable operational groupings reused across Teams.

**Concern:** grouping may be merely configuration subordinate to assignments/sessions. The product purpose does not itself require reusable evaluator groups.

### C4 — Evaluation Occurrence / Judging Session

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-04, P-05, P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** preserve a bounded historical occurrence in which a competitor was judged under a particular context, including who actually participated and what contextual snapshots applied.

**Why it may be a Concept:** substitutions, invalidation/rejudge and historical truth create strong occurrence-level behavior.

**Concern:** the current `Panel + Team` framing may be too specific. A session/occurrence candidate should be tested independently of reusable Panel membership.

### C5 — Evaluation Assignment / Obligation

**Purpose pressure:** P-03, P-04, P-05, P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** represent that a particular evaluator or evaluator set is expected/permitted to evaluate a particular competitor under a declared basis, with pending, reassigned, waived, recused, satisfied or invalidated consequences.

**Why it may be a Concept:** Judges need to know what work remains; Organizers need to see unresolved obligations, substitutions and gaps. Those needs are currently spread across Panel membership, Encounter participation, Scorecard obligation creation, readiness and coverage.

**Concern:** an obligation may be derivable synchronization state rather than independently operated behavior. This is a strong challenger specifically because current design distributes the behavior widely.

### C6 — Evaluation Work Item (merge challenger)

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-04, P-05.

**Behavior sketch:** combine assignment/obligation, judging occurrence and resulting individual judgment into one user-facing work item.

**Why explore it:** tests whether the current occurrence/obligation/judgment decomposition is over-factored.

**Concern:** likely conflates shared historical context with independent Judge authorship; still useful as a counter-decomposition for 010-E/F.

## D. Operational completion, exceptions and outcome formation

### D1 — Readiness / Permission-to-Proceed

**Purpose pressure:** P-04, P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** answer whether a consequential operation may legitimately proceed based on current source state and policy.

**Why it appears candidate-like:** users see meaningful ready/not-ready states.

**Concern:** current evidence strongly suggests a pure derived projection with no independent authoritative state or user actions. This remains a useful negative candidate.

### D2 — Evaluation Sufficiency

**Purpose pressure:** P-02, P-04, P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** represent whether enough qualifying evaluation evidence exists for a Team/competition purpose, including explicit accepted exceptions that alter permitted consequence without fabricating evidence.

**Why it may be a Concept:** the distinction between missing evidence, satisfied evidence, and governed exception materially affects Team treatment and outcome eligibility; Organizers reason about and resolve it directly.

**Concern:** the sufficiency status may still be a derived policy projection. The exception behavior may belong to policy/governance rather than the candidate itself.

**Disposition at discovery:** strong challenger for behavioral specification; current `Coverage` mechanism classification is not accepted without re-test.

### D3 — Result Calculation / Aggregate

**Purpose pressure:** P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** numerically combine qualifying individual judgments under declared policy.

**Why it appears candidate-like:** organizers consume the resulting score.

**Concern:** current behavior is deterministic derivation from authoritative inputs, with no independent user-authored lifecycle or meaningful operations beyond calculate/query. Strong negative Concept candidate.

### D4 — Comparative Ordering / Rank

**Purpose pressure:** P-02, P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** order eligible competitors according to declared comparison/tie policy.

**Why it appears candidate-like:** ranking is visible and consequential.

**Concern:** current behavior is derived and non-editable; correcting the order means correcting evidence, eligibility, classification or policy. Likely mechanism/projection rather than Concept.

### D5 — Recognition / Award

**Purpose pressure:** P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** define and confer explicit recognition, including discretionary recognition not reducible to derived rank.

**Why it may be a Concept:** user-authored definitions/selections and independent recognition semantics create behavior beyond pure calculation.

**Concern:** definition versus conferral might eventually separate, but no strong rediscovery evidence currently requires that split.

### D6 — Reconciliation / Resolution

**Purpose pressure:** P-04, P-06, P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** make outcome-affecting discrepancies, exceptions or unresolved source conditions explicit; allow governed resolution/acceptance while preserving what the source condition actually was.

**Why it may be a Concept:** Organizers intentionally enter, inspect and resolve a recognizable post-judging process; resolution may have durable history and authority.

**Concern:** if every item is merely a view over other Concepts and policy, Reconciliation may remain process/mapping rather than independent behavior. Current documentation describes it as a work mode, which is exactly what must be tested rather than assumed.

**Disposition at discovery:** strong challenger for behavioral specification.

### D7 — Official Outcome / Outcome Declaration

**Purpose pressure:** P-06, P-07, P-09.

**Behavior sketch:** explicitly establish a reconstructible official statement of competition outcomes from a declared evidence/policy basis; later correction creates an explicit successor while preserving the previous official state.

**Why it may be a Concept:** calculated results are not official until a deliberate authority-bearing action occurs; official revisions have identity, history, affected/superseded semantics and user-visible consequences.

**Concern:** may be adequately owned by Competition finalization plus revision/history support. Must test whether the official outcome is a recognizable independently operated subject or merely a snapshot emitted by Competition.

**Disposition at discovery:** strong challenger for behavioral specification; current `Official Outcome Revision` mechanism classification is not protected.

### D8 — Outcome (monolithic challenger)

**Purpose pressure:** P-06.

**Behavior sketch:** combine sufficiency, aggregate, ordering, Awards, reconciliation and official declaration under one `Outcome` concept.

**Why explore it:** provides a deliberate counterexample to fragmented outcome semantics.

**Concern:** almost certainly a catch-all with several different purposes, authorities and derivation modes. Retained only as a negative decomposition to prevent accidental re-creation through later convergence.

## E. Correction, authority history and provenance

### E1 — Revision / Version History

**Purpose pressure:** P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** preserve successive authoritative states of a subject without rewriting earlier authority.

**Why it may be a Concept:** users explicitly amend evolving authoritative artifacts and need addressable historical states.

**Concern:** may be better as a generic supporting concept or concept-owned intrinsic revision behavior rather than a MUDAC-wide Concept.

### E2 — Origin / Provenance

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-07, P-09.

**Behavior sketch:** preserve meaningful origin, represented author/authority, capture actor/source, correction reason and transformation history needed to explain authoritative state.

**Why it may be a Concept:** paper capture and correction make actor/source/authority distinctions user-significant.

**Concern:** could be metadata/history subordinate to the concepts it explains if it has insufficient independent user behavior.

### E3 — Authority History (merge challenger)

**Purpose pressure:** P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** combine revision snapshots and origin/authority lineage into one concept answering both "what authoritative states existed" and "how/why they arose."

**Why explore it:** tests whether `Versioning + Provenance` are conceptually over-separated.

**Concern:** content-state history and explanatory origin have different semantics and query needs; combining them may create a broad audit concept.

### E4 — Correction

**Purpose pressure:** P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** represent a correction request/process that changes current authority through amendment, invalidation, replacement or successor confirmation.

**Why it appears candidate-like:** correction is user-visible and consequential.

**Concern:** correction is likely a family of actions/synchronizations across owning concepts rather than one reusable subject. Retained as a negative/pressure candidate, not promoted.

## F. Continuity and recovery

### F1 — Draft / Preserved Work

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-03, P-05.

**Behavior sketch:** preserve work-in-progress before assertion of authority.

**Why it appears candidate-like:** users experience draft recovery and uncertainty.

**Concern:** draft is almost certainly intrinsic state of the authored judgment (and perhaps other concepts), not an end-to-end purpose of its own.

### F2 — Capture

**Purpose pressure:** P-01, P-05, P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** bring judgment originating in another medium/person's transcription into the system while preserving semantic author/source.

**Why it appears candidate-like:** paper capture has distinct actors and provenance.

**Concern:** capture is a path/action affecting Judgment + Provenance, not independently valuable once the judgment is correctly represented.

### F3 — Recovery / Continuity

**Purpose pressure:** P-03, P-05.

**Behavior sketch:** preserve/restore legitimate user work after interruption, uncertain commit, device loss, connectivity loss or paper fallback while avoiding duplicate authority.

**Why explore it:** resilient continuity is a first-class product purpose and is currently distributed across many owners.

**Concern:** the behavior is cross-cutting and may only make sense in terms of each affected Concept's state/authority. A generic `Recovery` concept risks becoming a technical workflow shell.

**Disposition at discovery:** reconsidered seriously but **not retained as an independent Concept candidate for 010-E by default**. 010-E must instead verify that retained concept specifications expose enough intrinsic draft/uncertainty/recovery semantics to satisfy P-05; reopen Recovery only if that fails.

## G. External representation and release

### G1 — External Representation

**Purpose pressure:** P-09, P-07.

**Behavior sketch:** generate and preserve a stable representation tied to an identified source state, purpose, audience/disclosure profile and currency relationship.

**Why it may be a Concept:** users intentionally externalize information and need to know what source truth a representation reflects later.

**Concern:** could be represented as a generated artifact subordinate to source Concepts if independent lifecycle/actions are too thin.

### G2 — Release / Publication

**Purpose pressure:** P-09, P-08.

**Behavior sketch:** deliberately make an identified representation available to a declared audience/channel; withdraw or supersede release explicitly while retaining historical release truth.

**Why it may be a Concept:** distribution authority is distinct from representation generation and has user-recognizable state/actions.

**Concern:** may be one action/state family within a broader External Representation concept rather than a separate Concept.

### G3 — External Representation & Release (merge challenger)

**Purpose pressure:** P-09.

**Behavior sketch:** one concept owns generation, audience, release, withdrawal and successor representation semantics.

**Why explore it:** tests whether the current `Export + Publication` split is over-factored.

**Concern:** combining source representation currency with distribution authority can recreate the exact ambiguity the current split was introduced to avoid.

# Material alternative decompositions

The divergent pass deliberately produced alternatives that allocate behavior differently, not merely differently named candidates.

## Alternative 1 — Occurrence-centric judging

```text
Evaluator Group
    + Competitor
        → Evaluation Occurrence
            → Individual Judgment(s)
```

This resembles the incumbent Panel / Judging Encounter / Scorecard structure but was independently rediscovered because historical occurrence, group reuse and independent authorship are distinct pressures.

**Strength:** strong historical truth and shared-context semantics.

**Risk:** work assignment/completion obligations are indirect and distributed.

## Alternative 2 — Assignment/obligation-centric judging

```text
Evaluation Assignment / Obligation
    evaluator(s)
    competitor
    evaluation basis
    required/waived/reassigned state
        ↓
Judging Occurrence (only when work actually happens)
        ↓
Individual Judgment
```

**Strength:** directly models "what remains to be done," substitution, recusal and missing work.

**Risk:** may duplicate state already derivable from Participation, grouping, occurrence and judgments.

## Alternative 3 — Evaluation work-item model

```text
Evaluation Work Item
    = assignment + occurrence + judgment workflow
```

**Strength:** simple user-facing task mental model.

**Risk:** collapses shared occurrence context and independently authored Judge judgments; likely fails specificity/independence.

## Alternative 4 — Actor-context model

```text
Actor Context
    = identity continuity
    + competition capacity
    + current capability/disclosure
```

**Strength:** simple "who I am here" mental model.

**Risk:** permanent identity, temporal participation and resource/time-specific authorization change for different reasons and may need different history.

## Alternative 5 — Split authority model

```text
Identity
  ↓
Participation
  ↓
Contextual Capability / Disclosure
```

**Strength:** preserves distinct reasons for continuity, capacity and permission.

**Risk:** may be conceptually over-factored if ordinary users never operate the pieces independently.

## Alternative 6 — Derived outcome pipeline

```text
Judgments
  → Sufficiency projection
  → Aggregate projection
  → Rank projection
  → Competition Finalization
  → official snapshot
```

**Strength:** keeps deterministic calculations as mechanisms.

**Risk:** may under-model user-visible reconciliation and official-outcome succession.

## Alternative 7 — Outcome-authority model

```text
Evaluation Sufficiency
      + calculations
      + Reconciliation
          → Official Outcome
              → Recognition / external representation
```

**Strength:** gives explicit semantic homes to evidence sufficiency, resolution work and official declaration.

**Risk:** could create too many outcome-layer Concepts whose state is mostly derived.

## Alternative 8 — Unified Outcome concept

```text
Outcome
  = sufficiency + aggregate + rank + award + reconciliation + officiality
```

**Strength:** apparently simple.

**Risk:** conflates derivation, user decision, exception handling and authority. Kept only as a negative alternative.

## Alternative 9 — Split history model

```text
Revision / Version History
    !=
Origin / Provenance
```

**Strength:** separates "what states existed" from "how/why/through whose authority they arose."

**Risk:** users may experience the pair as one history concept.

## Alternative 10 — Unified authority-history model

```text
Authority History
    = state lineage + origin + actor/source + reason
```

**Strength:** one explanatory history model.

**Risk:** catch-all audit semantics and weak independent behavior.

## Alternative 11 — Split representation/release model

```text
External Representation
    → Release / Publication
```

**Strength:** generation never implies distribution authority.

**Risk:** two Concepts may be excessive if release has too little behavior beyond state transition.

## Alternative 12 — Unified representation/release model

```text
External Representation
    generation + audience + publish/withdraw/supersede
```

**Strength:** one familiar artifact/release lifecycle.

**Risk:** conflates source currency and distribution state.

- `Division` — meaningful classification behavior exists, but independent Concept status and genericity remain conditional;
- `Panel` — reusable evaluator grouping is plausible, but assignment-centric alternatives can make it subordinate configuration;

## New or newly serious challenger candidates

The rediscovery produces four especially strong challenger candidates not currently in the accepted Concept catalog:

1. **Evaluation Assignment / Obligation** — because remaining work, substitution, recusal, waiver and missing evaluation are central user concerns currently distributed across several owners;
2. **Evaluation Sufficiency** — because missing-versus-sufficient evidence and accepted exceptions are consequential user-facing semantics, not merely numerical calculation;
3. **Reconciliation / Resolution** — because Organizer resolution work may have enough durable semantic state/action identity to exceed a mere work mode;
4. **Official Outcome / Outcome Declaration** — because explicit official authority and successor correction may constitute an independently recognizable behavior rather than only a Competition snapshot.

These are **retained for specification pressure**, not promoted Concepts.

## Merge/reframe challengers carried into 010-E/F

The following alternatives must remain visible during specification and specificity testing:

- `Actor Context` versus `Identity + Participation + Access`;
- `Evaluation Work Item` or `Evaluation Assignment` versus `Panel + Judging Encounter + obligation effects`;
- `Authority History` versus `Versioning + Provenance`;
- unified `External Representation & Release` versus `Export + Publication`;
- broader `Evaluation Occurrence` versus the current `Judging Encounter` identity tied specifically to Panel + Team.

Reassessed and still a strong derived-mechanism candidate. Ordering is consequential but remains determined by eligible inputs and comparison/tie policy.
